VULTURE — Full Mission Brief

Autonomous Sensing & Tracking Satellite Demonstrator

1. Mission objective

The primary objective is to develop and demonstrate a satellite capable of:

  • Reaching space and operating in a space environment.
  • Autonomous observation and sensing.
  • Identifying and tracking objects or visual targets within its observation capability.
  • Performing onboard processing rather than relying entirely on Earth-based processing.
  • Operating through programmed mission logic and autonomous decisions.
  • Sending telemetry, status reports and selected mission information to a ground station.
  • Demonstrating a progression from a conceptual prototype to an actual space-based experiment.

2. Mission philosophy

Build small. Test honestly. Learn continuously. Reach space.

The project does not pretend that a prototype is already a flight-ready spacecraft. Every stage is intended to produce practical evidence, lessons and improvements. The team will progress through design, prototype construction, testing, validation and future launch preparation.

3. Autonomous operation

The satellite is intended to perform selected operations without constant human intervention. Operational logic will progressively support:

  • Autonomous startup and operational states.
  • Observation and sensing.
  • Processing acquired information.
  • Mission-defined tracking functions.
  • Decision-making according to programmed rules.
  • System health monitoring.
  • Fault-aware operation and recovery.
  • Communication of selected information to Earth.

The degree of autonomy will evolve as the project develops and is validated.

4. Observation and tracking

VULTURE is conceived as a sensing and tracking demonstrator. Its observation capability is intended to explore how a satellite can acquire visual information, interpret observations onboard and perform mission-defined tracking tasks. The project will progressively investigate limitations and capabilities of its observation system, including accuracy, processing capability, environmental conditions and operational reliability. Exact tracking targets and performance capabilities remain subjects of future development and testing.

5. Onboard intelligence

Instead of treating the satellite merely as a remotely controlled device, the project intends to investigate how it can process observations locally, execute predefined mission logic, respond to operational conditions, monitor its own system state, prioritise selected information, and reduce unnecessary dependence on continuous human intervention. This is an evolving experimental capability, not a claim of advanced AI already operating onboard.

6. Communication and ground station

The ground station will serve as the satellite's connection to Earth. Planned interaction focuses on receiving telemetry, monitoring mission health, receiving selected observation data, sending authorised commands, reviewing mission logs, and supporting troubleshooting. VULTURE is not a broadband communications satellite. Its communication capability supports sensing, monitoring and mission operations.

7. Development journey

1
Concept & design
Establish the mission concept, objectives and initial design assumptions.
2
Prototype construction
Develop the physical prototype and explore the satellite's structural design.
3
Prototype testing
Perform initial structural tests, including planned drop, vibration and sound testing.
4
Electronics & firmware validation
Progressively validate the onboard computing, sensing, software and operational behaviour.
5
Mission simulation & integration
Integrate subsystems and test mission workflows under controlled conditions.
6
Space readiness
Investigate the requirements, validation, partnerships and launch pathways necessary for a future space mission.

These are development stages, not a claim that any stage beyond current prototype testing has been completed.

8. Current status

VULTURE is currently in its early prototype and development stage. The project has begun initial firmware testing, successfully demonstrating repeated boot sequences and camera frame acquisition in its current bench setup. The team is now progressing toward structured testing of the physical prototype.

Current milestone: Initial firmware baseline repeatability — 10 successful runs recorded.
The physical prototype is undergoing progressive development, with further tests planned before advancing toward more integrated hardware testing.

9. The team

VULTURE is currently being developed by a team of four participants, bringing together practical experience, experimentation, engineering interest and collaborative problem-solving.
